‎*[Anacardiaceae]*

‎that an oil is obtained from mastic, as from other kinds of resin (صمغ [ṣamgh] צמגים) (Ki tissa [=?] 76<sup>b</sup>).

‎Tournefort [=Tournefort, Relation d'un voyage du Levant] (st. [=died] 1708. Tschirch [=Tschirch, Handbuch der Pharmakognosie] 1, 906) reported on the harvesting of mastic on the island of Chios (Scio).

‎Arabic مصطكى [muṣṭakā] is a loanword (Fraenkel [=Fraenkel, Die aramäischen Fremdwörter im Arabischen] 151); it also migrated further into Armenian (Hübschmann [=Hübschmann, Armenische Etymologie] 248. ZDMG. [=Zeitschrift der Deutschen Morgenländischen Gesellschaft] 51, 304) and into the European languages.

‎Ever since Luther [=Martin Luther, German Bible translation] had rendered צרי*) in Ez. [=Ezekiel] 27, 17 by mastic, this was frequently repeated and is still retained by Rothstein [=Rothstein, translation of Ezekiel in Kautzsch] in the Kautzsch Bible (on <sup>c d e</sup>) and by others. <i>ṣŏrī</i> is not mastic but storax (Flora [=Löw, Die Flora der Juden] 3, 390), as Raši [=Rashi] on <sup>a</sup>) quite correctly states. In accordance with our usage one translates balsam (Rothstein), just as Abulwalīd [=Abulwalīd ibn Ğanāḥ, Book of Hebrew Roots] paraphrastically gives „<i>tirjāḳ</i>“, and as does the Geonic explanation in Ibn Esra [=Abraham Ibn Ezra], which understands by it a remedy compounded of 75 ingredients — that is, a theriac.
