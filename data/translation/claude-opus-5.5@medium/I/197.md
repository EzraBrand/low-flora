In Arabic P. Lent. is also called ضرو [ḍirw]. Nuwairī [=an-Nuwairī, Nihāyat al-arab] in E. Wiedem. [=Eilhard Wiedemann, Beiträge zur Geschichte der Naturwissenschaften] 49, 21 says that <i>kamkām</i> is the lentiscus resin of the tree <i>ḍirw</i>, according to others its leaves or bark. It flows out elastic, black as pitch. The tree resembles the terebinth, <i>buṭm</i>, according to others a very large oak, but is said to be smoother and more delicate. It bears clusters of fruit, like the <i>buṭm</i>. Used for toothpicks: Silberberg, Dināwarī [=Silberberg, Das Pflanzenbuch des ad-Dīnawarī] 78.

The midrash cited renders the bibl. [=biblical] <i>lōṭ</i> as mastic: מסטוכי (LA. [=variant reading] מסטכי, מסתיכי, corrupt. [=corrupted] מפטדי, correctly RDK. Menaḥem b. Salomo [=Menaḥem ben Salomo, author of Sechel tob] (1139) says: mustichi is like frankincense and consists of small grains like grains of salt.
