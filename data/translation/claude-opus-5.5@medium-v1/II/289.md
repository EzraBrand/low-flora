*[Olea]*

*[289]*

[it is stated] that this is used "in Mishnaic Hebrew as a technical term for the olive harvest", for this word occurs in Mishnaic Hebrew only at Sifre II 284, 124<sup>b</sup>, and then only followed by זיתיהם [zêtêhem].

Agricultural verbs, some of which, to be sure, are also used of other kinds of fruit, are e.g. אבק [ʾbq] Pi., to cover the root with earth (Goldm. 13); בקע [bqʿ] Hif., to hack off (Goldm. 17 n. 6: "to prune in order to increase the yield" corresponds to the commentaries on Švi. 4, 5, but here Guisius, citing T. Švi. III 65 <sub>8</sub>, correctly has: qui oleam radiciter effoderit); ברך [brk] Hif., to propagate by layering; גזם [gzm] Pi., T. Švi. I 62 for גמז [gmz], גמם [gmm] Qal, גרגר [grgr]<sup>1</sup>) to let ripen fully (Goldm. 31); דלל [dll] Hif., to thin out what grows too densely; זהם [zhm] Švi. 2, 4, Az. 50<sup>b</sup> to manure (Gaon. Cassel 39<sup>b</sup>), but RḤ.: to water with liquid manure; Raši: to smear tree wounds with dung. BJ. is incorrect: to coat with fat. זרד [zrd] Pi., to remove fresh shoots (see Rosaceae); חלק [ḥlq] Hif., to weed larger areas; יבל [ybl], of uncertain meaning; כרך [krk] to wrap the tree (Goldm. 14 n. 4); לקט [lqṭ], to pick singly (Goldm. 20); סקל [sql] to clear of stones T. Švi. I 62 <sub>2</sub> (13); סקר בסיקרא [sqr bsyqrʾ], to paint red (15); סתת [stt]? T. Švi. Kel. Bk. IV 573 <sub>4</sub> (50).

Galilee had the greatest abundance of oil. According to the blessing of Moses, Asher dips his foot in oil, and even in the Talmudic period the proverb ran: it is easier to feed a legion with olives in Galilee than a single child in Judaea (B. r. 20, 6).
