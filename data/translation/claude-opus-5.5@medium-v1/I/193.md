‎*[Pistacia Terebinthus]*

‎*[193]*

‎<i>el-buṭm</i>). The press residues are called in Jewish Aramaic כוספא דבטמי [kwspʾ dbṭmy] (Hg. 58<sub>5. 29</sub>).

‎Guigues p. XXXIX says: The green grains, granum viride (الحبّة الخضراء [al-ḥabba al-khaḍrāʾ]) of the Middle Ages, are the fruits of the terebinth, P. Tereb. L. They have been confused with pistachios, <i>fusṭuḳ</i>, fruits of P. vera L. Moreover, the green grains are still used as food today. It is above all in Mesopotamia that the fruits of the terebinth, <i>buṭm</i>, are edible.

‎בטנה [bṭnh], <i>bŏṭnā</i>,*) [Assyr. <i>buṭnu</i>, Zimmern AF. 54; Palmyr. בר בוטן [br bwṭn]**) n. pr. Chajes, Onomatolog. 12] also Mishnaic. Švi 7, 5: בוטנא [bwṭnʾ], variant reading בטנה [bṭnh] (TŠvi V 68<sub>14</sub>, Schwarz p. 215), once also Targ. בוטנין [bwṭnyn] Gn. 43, 11.

‎<i>boṭnā</i> is a native tree, whereas pistachios are imported from abroad (j Dem. II 22<sup>b</sup><sub>69</sub>). These come to Palestine from northern Syria (Rosen, Pf. 69).

‎The Mishnah and Tosefta passage cited speaks of young, edible shoots, לולב [lwlb]. The following are named: a) זרדים [zrdym], b) הרובים [hrwbym], c) אלה [ʾlh], d) בוטנה [bwṭnh], e) אטדין [ʾṭdyn], f) אדיל [ʾdyl]. c) according to Maimūnī, correctly, <i>ballūṭ</i>,***) i.e. <i>ʾallā</i> oak, not <i>ʾēlā</i>! Gal. VI 644 βλαστοὶ τερμίνθου [blastoi terminthou]! e) ῥάμνου [rhamnou]. He says: the terebinth shoots are pickled in vinegar or brine (Sachau, Inedita 95).

‎The place name <i>bŏṭnā</i> alone proves that the foreign pistachio cannot be meant. <i>buṭm</i> occurs as a place name in Arabic: Socin, Ortsapell. ZDPV. 22, 18: <i>challet il-buṭm</i> terebinth valley 31, 199, <i>maḳāṭiʿ el-buṭmi</i> terebinth quarry (31, 183).

‎בטנה [bṭnh] Jos. 13, 26, according to Robinson 3, 924 <i>baṭne</i> south of <i>eṣ-ṣalt</i> (Schwarz, Tebuot haareṣ ed. Luncz, p. 271 n). A place <i>boṭnā</i> also jAz. I 39<sup>d</sup><sub>27</sub> = בטנן [bṭnn] B. r. 47 end. The fair at the terebinth ירידה של בוטנה [yrydh šl bwṭnh] (ZAW. 29, 217. 221). S. Klein has combined with this the market of בית האילם [byt hʾylm] MT. 185<sub>17</sub> Hoffm., בית אילנים [byt ʾylnym] Sifre II 306, 132<sub>6</sub> Friedm.

‎Dérenbourg has connected with the place name <i>bŏtnā</i> בטנית [bṭnyt] <i>bŏṭnīt</i>, a female proselyte from <i>boṭnā</i> (Sabb. 24, 5. Beza 3, 8. T. III 205<sub>27</sub>. TMen XIII 533<sub>33</sub>. [Pes. 57<sup>a</sup>] Ned. 23<sup>a</sup>. M. Zizit p. 23<sub>6</sub> Kirchheim = Semaḥ. XII [p. 98<sub>12</sub> Romm. for קטנים [qṭnym] read, with M. Zizit, Šibbŏlē 341 Buber and R. Elijah of Vilna, בטנית [bṭnyt]. ZAW. 29, 216).
