‎*[196]*

‎*[Anacardiaceae]*

‎that an oil is obtained from mastic, as from other kinds of resin (صمغ [ṣamgh] צמגים [ṣmgym]) (Ki tissa 76<sup>b</sup>).

‎Tournefort (d. 1708. Tschirch 1, 906) reported on the production of mastic on the island of Chios (Scio).

‎Arabic مصطكى [maṣṭakā] is a loanword (Fraenkel 151); it has also migrated further into Armenian (Hübschmann 248. ZDMG. 51, 304) and into the European languages.

‎For Arabic: E. Wiedem. LVI 340. <i>mastik</i> (P.) Vullers 2, 51 = رَمَاسى [ramāsī]. מצטכי [mṣṭky] Abulw. 302 Bacher. Sha 1879, Syriac كيا [kyā] 1756. Physicians: Sha loc. cit. Kašef = كيا [kyā]. Abu Salt: משטיגי [mšṭygy]. Estori ha-Farḥī writes מצטכא [mṣṭkʾ] and, in the name of his father, identifies it with קטף [qṭp] (f 421<sup>b</sup> Ven., 107<sup>b</sup> Edelm., p. 662 Luncz).

‎Mastic is adulterated with the resin of the <i>ʾubhul</i> (juniper): Ibn Bassām in E. Wiedem. XL 177. 189.

‎Romance forms among Jews: משתיק [mštyq] (Abulw. 302 Bacher), מסתיקי [mstyqy], מסתיצי [mstyṣy] Mb. 31. 50. Menaḥem b. Salomo (1139) says: <i>lōṭ</i> is the resin of the tree שטינקי [šṭynqy] (lentisco). In Italian it is called: משטיכו [mšṭykw]; it is a remedy and serves to strengthen the teeth (Sechel tob p. XI.). — Grünbaum, Jüd.-span. Chrest. 11 f.: <i>almastig, almaciga</i> משטיק [mšṭyq] (Semag lāwīn 65 f. 22<sup>a</sup>). On account of the blessings due for its fragrance, Tur and SchA OCh. 216, 3 mention it, in the codices as מצטכי [mṣṭky], in Zēdā lederech 64<sup>b</sup> as מצתכין [mṣtkyn], מצטיכי [mṣṭyky], מסטיכי [msṭyky].

‎Since Luther had rendered צרי [ṣŏrī]*) in Ez. 27, 17 as mastic, this was frequently repeated and is still retained by Rothstein in the Kautzsch Bible (on <sup>c d e</sup>) and others. <i>ṣŏrī</i> is not mastic but storax (Flora 3, 390), as Rashi on <sup>a</sup>) quite correctly states. In accordance with our usage one translates "balm" (Rothstein), just as Abulwalīd renders it periphrastically as „<i>tirjāḳ</i>“, and as does the Geonic explanation in Ibn Ezra, which understands by it a remedy compounded of 75 ingredients — that is, a theriac.

‎When Targ. j substitutes שרף (ו)קטף [śrp (w)qṭp] for it, it will mean storax. In any case Onkelos with שעיף [šʿyp], Samaritan שעבה [šʿbh] (variant readings שעפה [šʿph], שעבי [šʿby], שאבה [šʾbh]), means not mastic but probably storax, not "balm" in general.
