‎*[Anacardiaceae]*

‎that an oil is obtained from mastic, as from other kinds of resin (صمغ [ṣamgh] צמגים) (Ki tissa [=?] 76<sup>b</sup>).

‎Tournefort [=Joseph Pitton de Tournefort, Relation d'un voyage du Levant] (st. [=died] 1708. Tschirch [=A. Tschirch, Handbuch der Pharmakognosie] 1, 906) reported on the collection of mastic on the island of Chios (Scio).

‎Arabic مصطكى [maṣṭakā] is a loanword (Fraenkel [=Siegmund Fraenkel, Die aramäischen Fremdwörter im Arabischen] 151), and has also migrated further into Armenian (Hübschmann [=Heinrich Hübschmann, Armenische Grammatik I: Armenische Etymologie] 248. ZDMG. [=Zeitschrift der Deutschen Morgenländischen Gesellschaft] 51, 304) and into the European languages.

‎For Arabic: E. Wiedem. [=Eilhard Wiedemann, Beiträge zur Geschichte der Naturwissenschaften] 56, 340. <i>mastik</i> (P. [=George E. Post, Flora of Syria, Palestine and Sinai]) Vullers [=J. A. Vullers, Lexicon Persico-Latinum etymologicum] 2, 51 = رَمَاسى [ramāsā]. מצטכי Abulw. [=Abulwalīd Ibn Janāḥ, Kitāb al-uṣūl] 302 Bacher [=Wilhelm Bacher (ed.), Sepher haschoraschim of Abulwalîd]. Sha [=M. Steinschneider, Heilmittelnamen der Araber] 1879, Syriac كيا [kiyā] 1756. Physicians: Sha a. O. [=at the place cited] Kašef [=Kachef er-rumūz, transl. Leclerc] = كيا [kiyā]. Abu Salt [=Abū ṣ-Ṣalt Umayya of Denia, treatise on simple drugs]: משטיגי. Estori ha-Farḥī [=Estori ha-Farḥi, Kaftor waferaḥ] writes מצטכא and, in the name of his father, identifies it with קטף (f [=folio] 421<sup>b</sup> Ven. [=Kaftor waferaḥ, Venice edition 1549], 107<sup>b</sup> Edelm. [=Kaftor waferaḥ, ed. Edelmann], p. [=page] 662 Luncz [=Kaftor waferaḥ, ed. Luncz]).

‎Mastic is adulterated with the resin of the <i>ʾubhul</i> (juniper): Ibn Bassām [=Ibn Bassām, Arabic author on market supervision (ḥisba)] in E. Wiedem. 40, 177. 189.

[=ben] Salomo [=Menaḥem ben Salomo, author of Sechel tob] (1139) says: <i>lōṭ</i> is the resin of the tree שטינקי (lentisco). It is called in it. [=Italian]: משטיכו; it is a remedy and serves to strengthen the teeth (Sechel tob [=Menaḥem ben Salomo, Sechel tob, ed. S. Buber] p. 11.). — Grünbaum, Jüd.-span. Chrest. [=Max Grünbaum, Jüdisch-spanische Chrestomathie] 11 f. [=and the following]: <i>almastig, almaciga</i> משטיק (Semag [=Sefer Mitzwot Gadol of Moses of Coucy] lāwīn 65 f. 22<sup>a</sup>). On account of the eulogies due to its fragrance, Tur [=Arbaʿa Ṭurim of Jacob ben Asher] and SchA [=Shulḥan ʿArukh] OCh. [=Oraḥ Ḥayyim] 216, 3 mention it, in the codices as מצטכי, in Zēdā lederech [=Ṣēdā la-derekh by Menaḥem ibn Zeraḥ] 64<sup>b</sup> as מצתכין, מצטיכי, מסטיכי.

‎Since Luther [=Martin Luther, German translation of the Bible] had rendered צרי*) in Ez. [=Ezekiel] 27, 17 by mastic, this was frequently repeated and is still retained by Rothstein [=J. W. Rothstein, translation of Ezekiel in Kautzsch (ed.), Die Heilige Schrift des Alten Testaments] in the Kautzsch Bible (on <sup>c d e</sup>) and by others. <i>ṣŏrī</i> is not mastic but storax (Flora [=Immanuel Löw, Die Flora der Juden] 3, 390), as Raši [=Rashi (Solomon ben Isaac), commentary] on <sup>a</sup>) quite correctly states. In accordance with our usage one translates balsam (Rothstein), just as Abulwalīd [=Abulwalīd Merwān ibn Ğanāḥ, Book of Hebrew Roots] paraphrastically gives „<i>tirjāḳ</i>“, and the Geonic explanation in Ibn Esra [=Abraham Ibn Ezra, commentary], which understands by it a remedy compounded of 75 ingredients — that is, a theriac.
