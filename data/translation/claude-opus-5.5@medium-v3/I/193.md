‎*[Pistacia Terebinthus]*

‎*[193]*

‎<i>el-buṭm</i>). The press residues are called in Jewish [=Jewish] Aramaic [=Aramaic] כוספא דבטמי (Hg. [=Halachot gedolot, ed. Hildesheimer] 58<sub>5. 29</sub>).

‎Guigues [=P. Guigues, Le livre de l'art du Traitement] p. [=page] 39 says: The green grains, granum viride (الحبّة الخضراء [al-ḥabba al-khaḍrāʾ]) of the Middle Ages, are the fruits of the terebinth, P. Tereb. L. [=Linnaeus]. They have been confused with pistachios, <i>fusṭuḳ</i>, fruits of P. vera L. Moreover, the green grains are still used as food today. It is above all in Mesopotamia that the fruits of the terebinth, <i>buṭm</i>, are edible.

‎בטנה, <i>bŏṭnā</i>,*) [assyr. [=Assyrian] <i>buṭnu</i>, Zimmern AF. [=H. Zimmern, Akkadische Fremdwörter] 54; palmyr. [=Palmyrene] בר בוטן**) n. pr. [=nomen proprium] Chajes, Onomatolog. [=H. P. Chajes, Beiträge zur nordsemitischen Onomatologie] 12] also Mishnaic. Švi [=Mishnah tractate Shevi'it] 7, 5: בוטנא, LA. [=variant reading] בטנה (TŠvi [=Tosefta Shevi'it, ed. Zuckermandel] 5, 68<sub>14</sub>, Schwarz [=A. Schwarz's Tosefta editions and commentary] p. 215), once also Targ. [=Targum] בוטנין Gn. [=Genesis] 43, 11.

‎<i>boṭnā</i> is a native tree, whereas pistachios are imported from abroad (j Dem. [=Jerusalem Talmud, tractate Demai, ed. Krotoschin] 2, 22<sup>b</sup><sub>69</sub>). These come to Eretz Yisrael from northern Syria (Rosen [=?], Pf. [=Löw, Aramäische Pflanzennamen] 69).

‎The cited Mishnah and T. [=Tosefta, ed. Zuckermandel] passage speaks of young, edible shoots, לולב. The following are named: a) זרדים, b) הרובים, c) אלה, d) בוטנה, e) אטדין, f) אדיל. c) according to Maimūnī [=Maimonides, commentary on the Mishnah] correctly <i>ballūṭ</i>,***) d. h. [=that is] <i>ʾallā</i> oak, not <i>ʾēlā</i>! Gal. [=Galen, Opera omnia, ed. Kühn] 6, 644 βλαστοὶ τερμίνθου [blastoi terminthou]! e) ῥάμνου [rhamnou]. He says: the terebinth shoots are pickled in vinegar or brine (Sachau, Inedita [=Eduard Sachau, Inedita Syriaca] 95).

‎The place name <i>bŏṭnā</i> alone proves that the foreign pistachio cannot be meant. As a place name Arabic <i>buṭm</i> occurs: Socin, Ortsapell. [=Albert Socin, Arabic place appellatives] ZDPV. [=Zeitschrift des Deutschen Palästina-Vereins] 22, 18: <i>challet il-buṭm</i> terebinth valley 31, 199, <i>maḳāṭiʿ el-buṭmi</i> terebinth quarry (31, 183).

‎בטנה Jos. [=Joshua] 13, 26 according to Robinson [=Edward Robinson, Palästina] 3, 924 <i>baṭne</i> south of <i>eṣ-ṣalt</i> (Schwarz, Tebuot haareṣ [=Joseph Schwarz, Tevuot ha-Aretz] ed. Luncz, p. 271 n [=note]). A place <i>boṭnā</i> also jAz. [=Jerusalem Talmud, tractate Avodah Zarah] 1, 39<sup>d</sup><sub>27</sub> = בטנן B. r. [=Bereshit Rabba, ed. Theodor and Albeck] 47 end. The fair at the terebinth ירידה של בוטנה (ZAW. [=Zeitschrift für die alttestamentliche Wissenschaft] 29, 217. 221). S. Klein [=Samuel Klein, professor in Jerusalem] has combined this with the market of בית האילם MT. 185<sub>17</sub> Hoffm. [=Midrash Tannaim, ed. D. Hoffmann], בית אילנים Sifre 2, 306, 132<sub>6</sub> Friedm. [=Sifre, ed. M. Friedmann].

‎With the place name <i>bŏtnā</i> Dérenbourg [=Joseph Derenbourg, Essai sur l'histoire et la géographie de la Palestine] has associated בטנית <i>bŏṭnīt</i>, a female proselyte from <i>boṭnā</i> (Sabb. [=tractate Shabbat] 24, 5. Beza 3, 8. T. 3, 205<sub>27</sub>. TMen [=Tosefta Menahot] 13, 533<sub>33</sub>. [Pes. [=tractate Pesahim] 57<sup>a</sup>] Ned. [=tractate Nedarim] 23<sup>a</sup>. M. Zizit [=Massekhet Zizit] p. 23<sub>6</sub> Kirchheim [=Raphael Kirchheim, Septem libri Talmudici parvi Hierosolymitani] = Semaḥ. [=tractate Semahot] 12 [p. 98<sub>12</sub> Romm. [=Romm edition (Vilna) of the Talmud] קטנים read, with M. Zizit, Šibbŏlē [=Shibbole ha-Leket, ed. S. Buber] 341 Buber [=Salomon Buber] and R. [=Rabbi] Elia Wilna, בטנית. ZAW. 29, 216).
