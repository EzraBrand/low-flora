‎The identification of the fruit of the goodly tree with the <i>eṯrōg</i> is an undisputed tradition within rabbinic Judaism (Maim. [=Maimonides], Introduction to the Mischnakomment. [=Mishnah commentary] f. [=folio] 54<sup>a</sup> Romm [=Romm edition (Vilna)]; Maim. H. Lulab [=Hilkhot Lulav, Maimonides, Mishneh Torah] 7, 2; L. Löw gS. [=Leopold Löw, Gesammelte Schriften] 1, 280). The necessity of a Sinaitic tradition is proven precisely by the fact that the identification of the <i>eṯrōg</i> with the fruit required in the Bible cannot otherwise be justified (Leon da Modena, Bechinaṯ hadaṯ [=Leon da Modena, Beḥinat ha-Dat] 26). This is still emphasized — in accordance with the traditional view — by Chajim Sofer, rabbi of the orth. [=Orthodox] congregation in Budapest (Kōl Sofer [=Chajim Sofer, Kol Sofer], 1882, note on Abot [=Pirqe Abot] 3, 1). Maimūnī [=Maimonides] attempted a rational justification: the <i>eṯrōg</i> is suitable because it stays fresh throughout the entire duration of the festival, which cannot be said of peaches, pomegranates, quinces or pears (More [=Moreh Nevukhim] 3, 43 end). In point of fact this hardly holds true for pomegranates and quinces.

‎In Talmudic times the philological conscience sought aggadic supports for the equation <i>hādār</i> = <i>eṯrōg</i>; later, etymological justifications were sought for it. Nachmānī [=Nachmanides, Commentary on the Pentateuch] (Emor [=Parashat Emor] 106<sup>a</sup>; Meiri, Magen Abot [=Menahem ha-Meiri, Magen Avot] 111 Last; Rapoport, Erech Millin [=Rapoport, Erekh Millin] 253) ingeniously, but incorrectly, tries to extract from <i>eṯrōg</i> a root רגג, which is supposed to correspond to the hebr. [=Hebrew] הדר. For him the <i>eṯrōg</i> is the fruit of Paradise that arouses desire — רגג. It led Eve into sin; therefore, together with other plants, it is to serve for atonement. As late as 1819 M. J. Landau [=Moses Israel Landau, ed. of the Aruch] (Ar. [=Aruch, ed. Landau] 1, 231) attempts to derive etymologically “to gild,” thus golden fruit, and Kohut [=Aruch, ed. Kohut] asserts (1, 336; 8, 274) that p. [=Persian] ترنك [turunk] is synonymous with <i>hādār</i> (?) and wishes to find in this the justification of the traditional identification.

‎In the 13th century Eleazar of Worms calculated that the numerical value of פרי עץ הדר, 659, is the same as that of the word [א]תרוגים (Rokeach [=Sefer ha-Roqeaḥ by Eleazar of Worms] 39<sup>b</sup> Zolkiew).
